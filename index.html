Rocketversity Project Overview

What matters: Rocketversity was built as enterprise learning infrastructure, not merely a course repository. The report describes a customized Moodle environment intended to centralize organizational knowledge, standardize onboarding, and establish repeatable learning pathways for employees, interns, and Kampus Merdeka participants.

The operating model is especially important. It separates knowledge sources, curriculum architecture, learning assets, learning pathways, and organizational impact, while defining governance responsibilities for subject-matter experts, course coordinators, and L&D. That structure turns content maintenance from an informal activity into a managed system.

The strongest contribution is the knowledge-to-course pipeline: auditing distributed materials, standardizing course structures, organizing a learning taxonomy, and converting internal knowledge into reusable digital assets. The report also identifies a forward path toward competency-based learning, skills intelligence, analytics, adaptive experiences, and AI-assisted recommendations.

THRIVE OpenSRP Case Study

What matters: the case study documents two concrete implementation problems during digitization of clinical intake forms: inconsistent form completion among health workers and extreme latency in mobile XLSForm rendering. The work therefore combined human-process standardization with technical performance investigation.

The intervention used structured interviews to isolate interpretation problems, a health-department consensus process to standardize completion, and a field timing study to quantify rendering failures. Because the clinical content could not simply be changed, an interim technical mitigation decomposed long forms into shorter, functionally equivalent segments.

The reported outcome is measurable: baseline latency ranged from seconds to many minutes, with some forms failing to load, while the post-intervention forms generally loaded in under ten seconds and some in under five. The resulting evidence also gave consortium stakeholders a quantitative basis for a longer-term technology change.

Astra Lean Production System Laboratory Establishment Report

What matters: the report describes the creation of a 150–200 m² educational and research facility designed to bridge engineering theory and industry-standard lean-manufacturing practice. Its learning scope included production planning, logistics engineering, quality engineering, and six practical training modules.

The laboratory was designed around reconfigurability and observable operations. Modular assembly lines, ergonomic workstations, conveyors, a small warehousing system, barcode-enabled work instructions, and live line-status monitoring allowed different production scenarios to be tested rather than merely discussed.

The project also had a clear implementation arc: technical design and module development, physical assembly and trial use, formal establishment, and inauguration with the industry partner. The report frames the lab as both a learning environment and a research platform for topics such as JIT, Kanban, TPM, sustainable manufacturing, and human-machine systems.

Engineering Management Internship Program: 7-Year Retrospective

What matters: the report presents the internship as a three-phase academic operating system rather than a placement-only activity. Preparation starts well before deployment, industry execution is documented continuously, and the return phase requires formal reporting, seminar evaluation, revisions, and final clearance.

The program connects students with major national and multinational companies and uses real operational problems as the learning context. The report highlights production, process, and business-analysis work supported by tools such as statistical process control and value-stream mapping.

Assessment is distributed across company supervision, academic advising, examination, and administrative compliance, which reduces dependence on a single evaluator. The report also records a strong 2017 cohort result, but that metric is currently flagged in the backlog for confirmation before it is promoted more prominently on the site.

Engineering Management Curriculum Handbook & System Mapping

What matters: this handbook treats curriculum design as a complete institutional system. It connects expert interviews, employer fieldwork, graduate feedback, international benchmarking, regulatory requirements, course architecture, senate review, transition mapping, and student-information-system synchronization.

The curriculum architecture is built around a 144-credit, eight-semester pathway and a set of knowledge blocks spanning engineering fundamentals, quantitative analytics, socio-technical systems, operations, quality, information systems, business development, and professional ethics. It also explicitly maps gaps identified by employers and alumni back into curricular action.

The most operationally important elements are the governance and transition mechanisms. The handbook documents prerequisite logic, equivalency mapping between legacy and revised courses, senate review, and synchronization with the campus information system so that a curriculum change becomes an executable academic process rather than a static document.
